Introduction

Surgical diathermy (electrosurgery) uses high-frequency alternating current to produce a controlled thermal effect in tissue. The heat allows tissue to be cut and bleeding to be controlled during surgery.

The generator, active electrode, return path, activation control, alarms and safety monitoring work as one system, and they must be considered together.

Electrosurgery is different from electrocautery. In electrosurgery, current passes through the patient. In electrocautery, a heated element is applied to tissue and the patient is not part of the electrical circuit.

Study scope. This is an academic explanation, not an operating guide. Actual use depends on trained staff, the equipment manual and hospital policy.

Principle of surgical diathermy

Radiofrequency (typically several hundred kHz to a few MHz) is used instead of ordinary 50 Hz mains current because rapidly alternating current greatly reduces the risk of neuromuscular stimulation. It still heats tissue wherever current density and tissue impedance create enough power concentration.

A small active electrode concentrates the current at the treatment site. A large indifferent (dispersive) electrode spreads the return current over a wide area, so little heating occurs there.

  • SourceRF generator
  • FocusSmall active electrode
  • ReturnLarge indifferent electrode

I = V / R    P = V × I = I²R

Because heating follows I²R, it is greatest where current density is highest, which is at the small active electrode.

Machine construction and working

A diathermy unit contains an isolated power supply, an RF oscillator, logic and control circuits, a power output stage, an isolation arrangement, mode controls, active and return connections, hand or foot activation, and audio tone and alarm circuits. Working

The operator selects the mode and output power. When the hand switch or foot switch is pressed, the oscillator generates RF energy. The output stage delivers it through the chosen electrode circuit, and an audio tone signals that the output is active. The current returns to the generator through the indifferent electrode, and the alarms supervise the return path.

Output circuit arrangements

Output circuits may use direct connection to earth, capacitor coupling, or an RF-isolated output. These arrangements show why low-frequency leakage and high-frequency return paths must be considered separately. An isolated output reduces the chance of current finding an unintended earth return path through the patient.

Operating modes and waveforms

Surgical diathermy machines generate different waveforms for each mode. Early units used electron-tube circuits, while modern units are solid state.

Waveform character and tissue effect for each output mode
ModeWaveform characterEffect
CutRelatively continuous RFSupports vaporization and clean separation of tissue.
CoagulationInterrupted bursts with higher crest voltage and lower average dutyHeats tissue with less cutting; haemostasis.
BlendCut waveform that is interruptedCutting with added haemostasis.

Exact voltage, power and modulation depend on the equipment.

Modern and automated systems

Modern units may use microprocessor control, tissue-response sensing, voltage control and spark-intensity control. Some generators adjust their output as tissue dries or its impedance changes, which improves consistency. Settings are still not universal across manufacturers.

Soft, forced and spray coagulation are different coagulation modes. The exact energy, distance and clinical use must come from the generator's own labelling and manual.

Soft, forced and spray coagulation modes
ModeDescription
SoftContact coagulation with controlled tissue heating.
ForcedMore visible sparking and faster coagulation.
SprayNon-contact surface coagulation through arcing.

Monopolar vs bipolar techniques

In the monopolar technique, current flows from the generator through the active electrode, the target tissue and the patient, then returns through the dispersive electrode. In the bipolar technique, both electrode functions are at the instrument tip, so current is concentrated between the two jaws or tips.

Comparison of monopolar and bipolar techniques
FeatureMonopolarBipolar
Current pathGenerator → active electrode → patient → dispersive electrode → generatorBetween the two electrodes at the instrument tip
Return electrodeRequiredUsually not required for conventional bipolar use
Main advantageVersatile cutting and coagulationMore localized current path
Main concernReturn-pad contact and alternate current pathsCorrect connection, tissue sticking and local thermal spread

Electrodes

Cutting electrodes include needle, angulated lancet, wire loop, angulated band loop and straight lancet types. Fine electrodes give higher current density and are used for precise cutting.

Ball and plate electrodes have larger contact areas and are used for coagulation. Shape, contact area, tissue conductivity, activation time and electrode cleanliness all influence the result.

Safety depends on the whole perioperative team. The equipment manual, hospital policy and current safety guidance take priority over this summary table.

Surgical diathermy analyser

An analyser checks output power and electrical performance using a known non-inductive load. Current and voltage are measured, then an RMS conversion, a thermal converter or thermocouple, and a DC amplifier condition the result for display.

In a service test, the operator connects the output, selects the specified mode, measures the response and compares it with the permitted tolerance in the service procedure. A feedback or alarm function can flag an output outside the expected range.

Measurement chain

  1. Generator output
  2. Non-inductive load
  3. Current and voltage measurement
  4. RMS or thermal converter
  5. Display and feedback

Advantages, limitations and applications

Advantages

  • Rapid cutting and haemostasis.
  • Several electrode shapes and modes.
  • Can be monitored and feedback-controlled.

Limitations

  • Thermal spread is not zero.
  • Smoke and carbonization may obscure the field.
  • Settings vary between devices.

Applications

  • General and specialty surgery.
  • Endoscopic haemostasis.
  • Equipment testing and maintenance.

Conclusion

A surgical diathermy unit is a controlled RF energy system. Its clinical effect depends on waveform, electrode geometry, tissue response, current path and activation time.

Safe use requires the correct mode, a verified circuit, the lowest effective output, and continuous attention to alarms, return-electrode contact and fire risk.
