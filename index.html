01 / Introduction

Overview of Surgical Diathermy

Surgical diathermy, commonly called electrosurgery, uses high-frequency alternating current to produce controlled thermal effects in tissue. Appropriate radiofrequency operation reduces neuromuscular stimulation compared with low-frequency current.

01

Cutting

Concentrated energy rapidly heats tissue near the active electrode, enabling tissue division.

02

Coagulation

Thermal energy promotes tissue protein denaturation and helps control bleeding.

03

Clinical Use

Electrosurgical systems are used across surgical specialties for tissue cutting and coagulation.

02 / Construction

Equipment and Major Components

The unit combines a power supply, RF generation circuitry, output controls, electrodes and protective monitoring systems.

Principal Components

  • Power supply: provides electrical input and conversion.
  • RF oscillator: generates the high-frequency signal.
  • Control circuit: manages output mode and activation.
  • Output stage: delivers the selected RF energy.
  • Active electrode: applies energy to the target tissue.
  • Return electrode: completes the circuit in monopolar operation.
  • Safety circuitry: provides monitoring and protective functions.
  • Footswitch or hand control: activates the selected output.

Electrodes

Cutting electrodes include needle, lancet and loop designs. Their geometry affects current concentration and the precision of the tissue effect.

Ball and plate electrodes provide larger contact areas and are commonly associated with coagulation. Contact area, tissue properties, activation duration and electrode condition influence the outcome.

The large dispersive plate used in monopolar operation completes the return circuit.

Monopolar and Bipolar Operation

In monopolar operation, current flows from the active electrode through the patient to a dispersive return electrode. In bipolar operation, current passes between the two tips of a bipolar instrument, such as forceps, through the tissue held between them.

03 / Working Principle

How Does It Work?

The generator delivers high-frequency alternating current through the active electrode. Current density and energy delivery influence tissue heating and the resulting surgical effect.

Energy Generation

Electronic circuitry produces a high-frequency electrical output suitable for electrosurgery.

Energy Delivery

The selected output is delivered through the active electrode to the intended tissue site.

Tissue Effect

Localized heating produces cutting or coagulation depending on waveform and operating settings.

Tissue effects depend on generator design, electrode geometry, tissue properties, contact conditions and selected settings.

08 / Safety

Safety and Maintenance

Correct setup, inspection and use according to the manufacturer's instructions are essential for safe operation.

Return Electrode Contact

Follow manufacturer guidance for return electrode placement and contact monitoring.

Electrode Inspection

Inspect cables, connectors and electrodes for damage before use.

Burn Prevention

Follow precautions for unintended current paths, contact burns and capacitive coupling where relevant.

Equipment Checks

Perform required inspections and preventive maintenance using approved procedures.

Activation Controls

Check hand and foot controls for correct operation and avoid unintended activation.

Manufacturer Instructions

Use compatible accessories and follow the equipment manual and institutional protocols.
